FIGURE CAPTIONS

Figure 1. Geographic coverage of the 33 eligible European electricity systems used in the renewable-aware demand-response experiment, grouped by the five geographic regions considered in the study.

Figure 2. Distribution of hourly renewable penetration across the evaluated European regions. The boxes represent the interquartile range, the central lines denote medians, and whiskers summarize the non-outlier range.

Figure 3. Mean renewable-alignment improvement across electricity systems, with 95% confidence intervals computed using electricity systems as the independent statistical units. Positive values indicate stronger temporal alignment of electricity consumption with wind and solar availability.

Figure 4. Regional heatmap of renewable-alignment improvement for PPO, SAC, TD3, and the rule-based controller. Values report the mean percentage improvement across electricity systems in each geographic region.

Figure 5. Distribution of weekly demand-flexibility utilization, expressed as the absolute shifted energy relative to total weekly electricity demand. The figure illustrates how aggressively each controller uses the available demand-response flexibility.

Figure 6. Mean physical load-shift action across five levels of renewable availability. Positive actions restore or increase flexible demand, whereas negative actions defer demand. A renewable-aware policy should systematically adapt its actions as renewable availability changes.

Figure 7. Representative weekly operation for an electricity system with median renewable penetration. The upper panel shows original demand and available wind-plus-solar generation, while the lower panel shows the controller-specific demand adjustment relative to the original load.

Figure 8. Relationship between system-level renewable penetration and renewable-alignment improvement for the three DRL controllers. Trend lines and Pearson correlation coefficients indicate whether benefits increase in systems with greater renewable availability.

Figure 9. Mean deferred-demand backlog across the 168-hour evaluation horizon. Lower values and complete restoration near the end of the episode indicate more operationally feasible demand-response behavior.

Figure 10. Operational feasibility diagnostics covering action saturation, constraint violations, and final deferred demand. These indicators complement reward-based performance by exposing unrealistic or unsafe control behavior.

Figure 11. Mean inference latency per control decision with 95% confidence intervals across electricity systems. This metric assesses whether the evaluated controllers are computationally practical for near-real-time demand-response operation.
